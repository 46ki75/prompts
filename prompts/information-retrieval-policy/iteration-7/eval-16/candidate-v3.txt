The 2008 financial crisis is generally dated to begin in 2007, with the acute phase erupting in 2008. Key milestones:

- February–April 2007 — Early signs as U.S. subprime mortgage lenders (e.g., New Century Financial) began collapsing.
- August 9, 2007 — Often cited as the formal start of the credit crunch, when BNP Paribas froze redemptions on three funds exposed to U.S. subprime mortgages, triggering a freeze in interbank lending.
- March 2008 — Bear Stearns collapsed and was acquired by JPMorgan Chase in a Fed-backed deal.
- September 15, 2008 — Lehman Brothers filed for bankruptcy, which is widely regarded as the moment the crisis became a full-blown global financial panic.

So depending on how "start" is defined: 2007 for the underlying credit crunch, or September 2008 for the global panic phase.
