The 2008 financial crisis is generally dated to the collapse of Lehman Brothers on September 15, 2008, which triggered the acute global phase of the crisis. However, its origins trace back earlier: the U.S. subprime mortgage market began deteriorating in 2007, with notable early events including the failure of two Bear Stearns hedge funds in June 2007 and BNP Paribas freezing redemptions on three funds on August 9, 2007 — a date many economists cite as the start of the credit crunch.
